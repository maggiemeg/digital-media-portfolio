Course Goals


1. Consider technology’s role in acts of making

Across my three webtexts, I worked directly with coding tools to see how the affordances and limits of each program shaped not just how I presented ideas, but how I thought through creating them in the first place. As someone whose background is in creating digital graphics and video-based projects, I was pushed more connect how technology can directly create art. Prior to this class, I hadn’t thought about how most if not all of digital works are just lines and lines of code; now I see websites, graphics, and even images in a new light.

2. Embrace experimentation

Each project put me in front of at least one tool I hadn't used before, whether it be GitHub or Visual Studio Code. Rather than starting with a fixed plan, I learned to prototype first and try new ideas as I went. My process shifted from needing to have the perfect design already in my head to seeing how my creative process flowed with each edit and addition I made. This really pushed me as my process before this class was much stricter and more rigid; I’m happy that these projects enlightened the creative side of me to be comfortable trying new things and it be okay if they don’t always work. That shift shows up most clearly in my second design, Web text experiment, where I felt total ease in trying different ideas as I sifted through W3Schools examples.

3.Engage with others in a workshop environment

Our workshops shaped my second project specifically with how I approached revisions. After seeing all the diverse ways my classmates interpreted our assignment, I pushed myself to revisit my own web text and asked myself how much effort I put in. When I realized my experimentation was lacking, I took a second attempt at creating a web text experiment. Workshop taught more about my peers, how their backgrounds impact their creative decisions, and how I can revise my own works based on their feedback.

4. Practice revision within and across media forms

Coming in with practically no coding experience, I’d be lying if I said some of these projects didn’t feel daunting. My Code and Technology Narrative got me associated with the Pitt Fuego template; however, with each web text, I got increasingly comfortable with Visual Studio Code and GitHub and got more experimental with my work. My second web text is perfect example of revision: after seeing my classmates designs in workshop, I felt like I didn't push myself creatively enough or followed through with a centralized idea. My second go around, I centralized my work around creating a Digital Scrapbook of recent creative inspiration I found on Pinterest. I was much happier with my revised piece, as it spoke more to my creative passion and experimentation.

5. Learn to present your work

Presenting my code narrative out loud pushed me to articulate not just what I made but why I included what I did. Getting to share how certain technologies from my upbringing impacted how I interact with newer technologies in my education and personal life. After seeing fellow students present their web text experiments, it pushed me to return to my work and take more risks rather than just cutting and pasting random code. Finally, I felt much more confident presenting my last project, the Multi-Page Web text, because I was proud of my design and the story behind it. Getting to share my designs with my fellow classmates pushes me to think outside the box creatively, experiment more, and accept criticisms for edits and future works.

Course Goals

1. Consider technology’s role in acts of making

Across the sound composition, podcast, photo/text collage, and photo essay, I saw how the tools I used shaped not only how I presented ideas, but how I thought through them in the first place. My background is in digital graphics and video, so working in sound pushed me to think about how audio can carry meaning the way an image does. Editing in Audacity made me aware of how every cut, layer, and crop is a choice. Now I notice those choices in the podcasts, images, and soundscapes I encounter every day.


2. Embrace experimentation

Each project put me in front of at least one tool or technique I hadn’t used before, such as Adobe InDesign. Rather than starting with a fixed plan, I learned to prototype first and let ideas develop as I went. I used to need a perfect vision in my head before I began. Now I let my process unfold with each edit and addition, and I’m comfortable when an idea doesn’t work. That shift shows most clearly in my photo/text collage, where I felt at ease trying different things and layouts to see what fit.


3.Engage with others in a workshop environment

Workshop directly shaped how I approached revision. Getting direct feedback on pieces that felt super experimental, with platforms I wasn’t entirely comfortable with, opened me up to taking those notes and revisiting my work. Seeing the many ways my classmates interpreted each assignment made me reconsider my own choices and the effort I’d put in. I specifically felt this during my workshop for our Podcasts, because within my own listening I didn’t notice how my music could be distracting, but having fresh ears tell me that, I made it a point to change my music per each segment of my podcast to appropriately fit the tone.


4. Practice revision within and across media forms

Moving between sound and photography meant that no single skill carried over perfectly, and some projects felt daunting at first. My photo/text collage was more rigid, but with each new medium I grew more comfortable taking creative risks. My photo essay is the clearest example of revision. I felt really comfortable taking on the idea of “the unplanned journey”, both with my essay’s theme and with my creative approach, and I made a work I was surprisingly more proud of than I thought I would be.


5. Learn to present your work

Presenting my work out loud pushed me to explain not just what I made but why I made it, whether that was the reporting choices behind my podcast or the ideas guiding my photo essay. Watching classmates present motivated me to return to my own work and take more risks. In this unit, I felt far more confident presenting because, regardless of how confident I may have felt with my first draft, I was proud of both the work and the story behind it. Sharing with classmates pushes me to think creatively, experiment more, and accept criticism that makes future work stronger.
